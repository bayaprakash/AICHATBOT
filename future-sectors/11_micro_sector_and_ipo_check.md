# 11) Micro-sector stocks and new IPOs – what I found and how to check them (18 Nov 2026)

Short version: **I found few verifiable micro-cap or new-IPO ideas in these themes. Most results were listing-day stories, not fundamentals.** Details below. Search results and trackers disagree in places, so confirm in the offer documents and exchange filings. Not investment advice.

## 1) Market backdrop for IPOs (read before any IPO)
- Axis Capital study (374 mainboard IPOs/FPOs, Jul 2020–Jan 2026): **180 were trading below their issue price**; 13% were down 10–25%, and 9% were down 25–50% since listing.
- FY26 reviews: roughly two-thirds of mainboard IPOs were below issue price (one count: 71 of 108). Early 2026 saw weak or negative average listing gains, though a later tracker shows 255 IPOs with an average +15% listing gain, so figures differ by cutoff date.
- Take-away: **a listing-day pop is not evidence of a good business.** Most of the money in IPOs has been made or lost in the first day, and many listings later trade below issue.

## 3) SME IPO risk (micro-cap IPOs are the highest-risk segment)
- SEBI cancelled its first SME IPO: **Trafiksol ITS** (fabricated vendor credentials); the company and promoters were barred for one year on 28 Aug 2026, with ₹1.05 cr in penalties.
- **Synoptics Technologies:** three promoters barred for siphoning over ₹19 cr of IPO proceeds through fake counter-parties.
- **Add-Shop E-Retail:** over 46% of reported sales across three years found fictitious.
- SEBI is probing six investment banks over IPO malpractice, and a 180-day rule has been discussed for 2026 (details unconfirmed).
- Grey-market premiums can be used to manufacture demand. Do not use them as research.

## 5) IPO / micro-cap checklist (use before applying or buying)
Score each; walk away on any hard stop.

**Business and numbers (from the RHP / annual report)**
1. Revenue concentration: any customer above 30%? (Avaada Electro: 89% from one group company → red flag.)
2. Use of proceeds: fresh issue for growth, or OFS/debt repayment? (Tonbo: 100% OFS.)
3. Valuation vs listed peers on FY26 and forward earnings.
4. Three years of operating cash flow versus profit. Fictitious sales usually show up as profit without cash.
5. Receivable days and inventory days rising faster than revenue.

**Promoters and intermediaries**
6. Promoter holding after IPO, lock-in length, and any pre-IPO placement at lower prices.
7. Lead manager track record: check whether their past IPOs are above issue price.
8. Auditor: size, tenure, and any recent change.
9. SEBI/ED/tax actions against promoters or the company.
10. Related-party transactions and loans to group entities.

**Demand and execution**
11. Order book with named customers, not generic claims.
12. Anchor investors: are they credible long-term funds or repeat flippers?
13. Pre-IPO selling by existing investors (heavy OFS = early backers exiting).

**Hard stops:** vendor/customer details that cannot be verified; revenue rising with cash flow flat; promoter proceeds-diversion history; lead manager under SEBI probe; price band that implies a P/E far above listed peers with no growth proof.

**Position sizing:** IPO and SME positions at 0.5–1% of portfolio each; do not average down; decide exit levels before applying.

## 6) Conclusions
- Best-documented new listing: **Tonbo Imaging**. Real business and a high-barrier niche, but the entry price was rich, financial data conflicts, and the company got no new capital. Wait for the first results as a listed company and a post-listing price that reflects them.
- Best-documented pending IPO: **Avaada Electro**. The 89% group-customer share and the oversupplied solar market make this a "wait for the price band and the RHP" case.
- **Garuda Aerospace:** no verified data. Revisit when the RHP is out.
