## 1) Sector comparison

**The sector:** CRDMO = Contract Research, Development and Manufacturing Organisation. A pharma or biotech company hires it to discover, develop and make the drug. Sai is a small-molecule specialist (chemical drugs, not antibodies).
- Global CRDMO market ~$250B ($90B research services, $160B manufacturing), expected to grow ~13% a year through FY27 (the CRO part ~10%, manufacturing ~14%), per the ABM IPO note citing the offer document.
- India's share is small: ~3.8% (2024) to ~4.7% by 2029E per an HDFC Securities report (cited by a search summary; could not read the original).

## 2) How they earn

**Business mix (revenue)**
| | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 | Q1 FY27 | Q2 FY27 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CDMO (development + manufacturing) | ~69% | ~68% | ~66% | 60% | ~64% (one source) / 62% H1 |

(Source: PL charts; Q2 mix from news summaries that differ slightly.) Q2 FY27 growth: **CRO +28%, CDMO +20%.** Segment margins are not disclosed.

**Revenue models (how a customer pays)**
1. **FTE contracts (full-time equivalents):** a customer rents a dedicated team of chemists/biologists billed per head per year. Predictable, mostly CRO.
2. **Project / fixed-fee and milestone work:** process development, analytical work, clinical-trial supply batches.
3. **Commercial supply:** once a drug is approved, Sai makes the active ingredient or intermediate in batches. Highest volume, linked to the drug's sales.
4. **Platform deals:** a 5-year partnership with Schrödinger (software-driven discovery) signed in FY23 gives CRO visibility, per PL.

**Cost structure FY26 (PL):** materials ~26% of revenue (gross margin ~74%), employees ~31%, other ~14%, EBITDA ~29%. Capital intensive: depreciation ₹167 cr, capex ₹633 cr incurred.

**Customers (FY26)**
- 300+ customers; 19 of the top 25 global pharma companies; >90% of revenue from repeat customers; large pharma = **49% of revenue** (28% in FY22).
- Top 1 / top 5 / top 10 customers: **12% / 37% / 54%** of FY26 revenue (Q4 FY26 call, secondary). PL's chart shows lower concentration through FY25, which suggests concentration rose in FY26, but I could not map the chart reliably. Check the annual report.
- ~97% of revenue from North America, Europe, Japan and the UK (forum summary of the annual report; unverified).

## 3) Products and product usage

Sai does not sell its own branded drugs. Its "products" are services and chemicals made for others.
| Product / service | What it is | Who uses it and why |
|---|---|---|
| **Discovery chemistry and biology (CRO)** | Designing and making test molecules, running biology, drug-metabolism and toxicology studies | Biotechs and large pharma without enough in-house labs; ~65% of discovery customers use integrated services |
| **Process and analytical development** | Finding a safe, scalable, cheap way to make a molecule; testing purity | Before clinical trials and launch, to make drug supply reliable |
| **Clinical supply and API manufacturing (CDMO)** | Making the active ingredient (and intermediates) in large reactors at Bidar and Hyderabad. 155 programmes from IND to commercial | Approved drugs need steady supply; customers keep a supplier they have qualified |
| **Newer modalities** | Peptides (including GLP-1 and macrocyclic), ADC linkers and payloads, oligonucleotides, PROTACs, lipids | New drug types that need specialist chemistry; ~5–7% of revenue today |
| **Formulation (planned)** | Oral-solid facility for Phase I/II, commissioning H2 FY27 | Customers want first-in-human API plus finished dose from one vendor |

**Therapy exposure FY26 (PL chart; labels hard to read, treat as approximate):** central nervous system around a quarter of exposure, oncology ~15%, plus cardiovascular, immunology and others.

**Pipeline:** 33 commercial molecules (a Q1 deck said 34; corrected to 33), 14 late-phase, 50+ at candidate/IND/Phase I–III, 5 molecules taken from discovery to market, 17 launches in 5 years (PL). 3 of 4 programmes planned for this year expected to enter commercial supply.

## 4) The story behind it

- **1999:** founded in Hyderabad by Dr K. Ranga Raju (now chairman) as a medicinal-chemistry partner to US biotechs.
- **2000s–2010s:** bought a Hyderabad API unit and a Bidar unit; added reactors; Bidar became US FDA-approved; added biology so it could offer integrated discovery.
- **2014–2018:** more Bidar capacity; US FDA and Japan (PMDA) inspections passed; labs set up in Manchester (UK) and Greater Boston (US).
- **2019–2023:** oncology/high-potency capability; Schrödinger partnership; doubled process-R&D capacity.
- **Ownership:** MD & CEO Krishna Kanumuri (joined 2004; PL lists him as Krishnam Raju Kanumuri). TPG held a large stake and sold out in 2025 (see below).
- **Dec 2024 IPO:** price ₹549, ₹3,043 cr (₹950 cr fresh, ₹2,093 cr sold by existing holders); ₹720 cr of fresh money repaid debt; listed at ₹650 (+18%). The ABM note called it "a pricy bet".
- **2025:** TPG sold about 10% in June (₹1,505 cr to funds including Nippon India MF and BlackRock) and its remaining ~14.7% (₹2,675 cr) in August, per bulk-deal reports. A private-equity exit is not by itself negative, but it means the early backer chose to leave.
- **FY26:** revenue +29%, EBITDA +56%, PAT +109%; debt almost gone.
- **Now:** **Oct 2026 shareholding (PL):** promoters 34.5%, mutual funds 29.5%, foreign institutions 19.7%, no promoter pledge.

### d) Volume and capacity (the real engine)
- Manufacturing capacity 700 kL → 1,150 kL by FY27E (+64%); first 225 kL Bidar block ready, in final quality trials; the second moved to Q3 FY28; ~450 kL more by FY28E.
- **Utilisation 67% in Q2 FY27**; a CDMO plant is considered fully used at ~70–75% (CFO). Adding 64% capacity while still at 67% means margins and returns depend on filling the new plant. Discovery capacity added in Q1 FY27 was reported sold out.
- Capacity is built ahead of demand ("potentials and possibilities", 24–36 month decisions), with no firm customer commitments given for new capacity.

### e) Pipeline conversion (leading indicators)
Commercial molecules 11 (FY22) → 33 (FY26); Phase III molecules 4 → 14 (PL chart); 6 late-phase molecules added in 15 months, 5 via large-pharma FTE relationships; new modalities 5–7% of revenue. **Watch:** molecules moving to commercial, revenue per commercial molecule, large-pharma share, and the peptide/ADC revenue disclosure.

### f) Valuation as expectations, not a single multiple
Price confusion: PL's CMP was ₹1,390 (21 Oct 2026; 52-week range ₹783–1,697). One page (Tijori, 17 Nov 2026) shows ₹1,788 and a P/E near 99×. That is a 29% gap; **verify the live price.** PL's FY26 EPS ₹16.5; FY27E ₹19.6; FY28E ₹24.9; FY29E ₹33.8.

| Price | Market cap (~) | P/E on FY26 / FY27E / FY28E / FY29E EPS |
|---|---|---|
| ₹1,390 | ₹29,500 cr | 84× / 71× / 56× / 41× |
| ₹1,788 | ₹38,000 cr | 108× / 91× / 72× / 53× |

**What you need to believe (my arithmetic, FY29 profit × exit P/E of 40×; FY26 PAT ₹355 cr):**
| Scenario | PAT CAGR FY26–29 | FY29 PAT | Implied market cap at 40× |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bear | 15% | ~₹540 cr | ~₹21,600 cr |
| PL base case | 26% | ~₹716 cr | ~₹28,600 cr |
| Bull | 30% | ~₹780 cr | ~₹31,200 cr |
Compared with ₹29,500 cr (at ₹1,390) or ₹38,000 cr (at ₹1,788), **even PL's base case at a 40× exit multiple gives little or negative return** without a higher exit multiple (~58× for 15% a year from ₹1,390 by Mar 2029; ~74× from ₹1,788). The price already carries the growth. Returns need beats or a premium multiple to persist.

## Bottom line
- **Strengths:** integrated model, sticky customers (>90% repeat), large-pharma traction, deleveraged balance sheet, margins up from 14% to 30% in four years, 33 commercial and 14 late-phase molecules, inspected sites.
- **Weaknesses / risks:** customer concentration (top 10 = 54%), single main manufacturing site (Bidar), 67% utilisation ahead of a 64% capacity jump, negative free cash flow and rising debt in FY27, lumpy quarters, undisclosed segment margins and contract values, valuation, ~97% overseas revenue (currency), patent-expiry exposure on innovator drugs, biotech funding cycles.
- **Not verified:** exact FY26 customer concentration trend, the Bidar inspection record in FDA databases, the "sole supplier" and "lost molecule" claims, CEO pay, live price.
