# 9) Beyond AI – problem-solving and deep-tech sectors (18 Nov 2026)

Files 1–8 covered grid, electronics, defence and the stocks you asked about. This file widens the net: **sectors that exist because a big problem needs solving**, not because of AI hype. Numbers come from web searches (vendor reports vary a lot), and company names are research candidates, not recommendations. I did not check prices, valuations or management for anything here.

## How to dig out sectors (problem-first method)
1. **List problems that are getting more expensive to ignore:** energy security, import dependence for critical materials, water scarcity, food/farm productivity, chronic disease (obesity, diabetes), cyber attacks, border security, ageing, climate damage, logistics cost.
2. **For each, find who is forced to pay** (government budget, regulated utilities, insurers, exporters). Forced payers make durable demand.
3. **Find the choke point:** the scarce input or step (a magnet, a licence, a certification, a patent expiry) where a few firms capture the profit.
4. **Check the trigger date:** policy approval, patent expiry, tender award, plant start. Early plays have a dated trigger the market hasn't priced.
5. **Check listed proxies:** many deep-tech sectors are mostly unlisted startups. If you can't buy it, note it and move on.
6. Run file 4 (priced-in tests) and file 6 (barrier/management scorecard) on any stock.

McKinsey's Tech Trends Outlook 2025 lists 13 frontier trends. It treats AI as a layer across the others and includes quantum, advanced robotics, bioengineering, space, and future-of-energy technologies (per secondary coverage; I could not read the report itself). So "what else beyond AI" is a real list, below.
